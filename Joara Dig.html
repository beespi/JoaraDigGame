Welcome to Joara

Joara was a Native town in the Catawba Valley near present-day Morganton, NC. In 1567 Spanish soldiers built Fort San Juan beside it. Archaeologists learn from where and how things are found, so careful tools matter more than speed.

Digging tools

Trowel: the archaeologist’s standard. Slow, controlled and safe.
Pickaxe: breaks hard soil fast, but can chip anything buried beneath. Use it only far from finds.
Brush: for the last thin layer. Too gentle for packed soil.
Pickaxe: one click breaks a block and loosens its neighbors, leaving tiny cracks. Trowel: hold the mouse down. Brush: drag gently, slowest but safe. Cracks near a find mean the pickaxe was too rough. Buy potions in the Shop (one per day) to dig faster or find rarer things.

Cleaning tools (Lab)

Soft brush: drag across loose dust.
Dental pick: click hard crust, one patch at a time.
Spray bottle: press and hold on caked mud to soften it.

The three locations

Fort San Juan (FSJ): iron, silver and trade goods left by Spanish soldiers.
Joara Village (JV): houses and daily life: pottery, stone tools, beads.
Mound & Council House (MC): the ceremonial center: prestige items like shell and copper.

Storage

Every bag is tagged with a site code and a sketch. In Storage, record where it was found and what it is. Wrong records don’t get catalogued, and only catalogued artifacts are filed into your collection albums.
